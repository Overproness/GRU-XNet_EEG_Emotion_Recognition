## Scientific and resource decision

A future fixed-catalogue benchmark can answer a restricted estimation question: for these reserved participants/materials and these frozen source states, what is the mean incremental absolute-error gain of distributed EEG plus context over matched context, with category medians and coverage controls fully reported? Its participant-level two-sided paired-t interval conditions on these films and states, assumes independent participant gains and an approximately suitable sampling distribution, and omits retraining, selection and arbitrary-film uncertainty. Convenience recruitment does not establish representativeness.

A positive interval would support incremental prediction by this particular recipe on this catalogue. A negative interval would support worse performance by this recipe on that catalogue. A zero-crossing interval would be inconclusive about superiority. None proves equivalence, absent useful EEG information, adequately optimized neural decoding, brain causality or performance on arbitrary new films. The two baseline comparisons remain mandatory; an improvement over matched context alone does not establish an improvement over the stronger practical category prior.

**The present evidence justifies no EEG-superiority claim and certifies no eight-family precision target.** An independent benchmark may make the current paper's empirical claim more credible, but its modest established methods and limited catalogue still constrain novelty.
