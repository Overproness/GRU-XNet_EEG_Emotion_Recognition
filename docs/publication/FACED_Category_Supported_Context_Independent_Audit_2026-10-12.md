## Selection support and remaining limits

The first diagnosis's crossed source-title folds remove the held assigned categories because there is one source title per category. This separately declared follow-up retains all source titles in training while holding out people. It qualifies a category-informed contextual selection recipe under the stated inference contract.

**Source selection therefore tests new people on familiar source titles.** It is not an estimate of unseen-title performance or evidence that category effects generalize beyond those films. With one source film per category, category and film identity—and some duration/order properties—are confounded during tuning. Full-versus-category differences cannot identify a causal duration or acquisition effect.

The follow-up was motivated by development observations and metadata after the first experiment. Its validation outcomes and both EEG anchors remain adaptively reused. Source-only comparator selection does not erase earlier validation-based EEG selection. The three dependent rotations are sensitivity panels rather than independent replications.

## Verified results

The category median has the smallest source-person held MAE among the five declared context families in each rotation: 1.2554, 1.3014 and 1.2633. These are source-selection scores, separate from validation performance.

On unfamiliar validation titles, positive gain means EEG reduces MAE relative to the source-selected category median:

| Rotation | Category median MAE | Spectral joint MAE | Spectral gain | EEGNet residual MAE | Neural gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1.2220 | 1.2588 | -0.0368 | 1.1907 | +0.0314 |
| 1 | 1.2316 | 1.1767 | +0.0549 | 1.3110 | -0.0794 |
| 2 | 1.3269 | 1.2111 | +0.1158 | 1.2633 | +0.0636 |

**All six unfamiliar-panel descriptive gain intervals include zero.** The spectral direction is favorable in two rotations and unfavorable in one; the neural direction also varies. This supports neither a confirmed EEG improvement nor a claim that EEG adds no information.

On familiar titles, spectral gains are -0.0365, +0.0049 and -0.0799. Neural gains are -0.0966, -0.0766 and -0.1852. The last neural panel's descriptive interval lies below zero, [-0.3498, -0.0143], while the other familiar intervals include zero. That is a panel-specific development observation; it does not establish a generally inferior architecture or independent negative-transfer result.

The stronger category-supported comparison prevents apparent improvements against the first stage's coarse-valence-selected baseline from being mistaken for an advantage over a fully supported contextual comparator. Preserve both stages and their different selection tasks.

## Audit boundary and scientific consequence

This public audit checks training identities, scores, selections, priors, targets, copied anchors and interval arithmetic. It does not independently refit Ridge, read its private coefficient/scaler arrays or rerun EEG inference. Numerical contextual predictions are supported by immutable executable code and the fit ledgers; their reported metrics are independently reproduced. No new EEG features, encoder, training schedule, whole-trial input or availability policy was tested in this stage.

The preceding person/film pairing diagnostic remains relevant: spectral person-matching degradation is directionally consistent on unfamiliar titles, but its intervals include zero; film swaps and neural matching effects are mixed. Combining these observations does not establish emotion-specific or causal EEG information.

The original report and both FACED manuscript packages remain unchanged. No protected confirmation outcome was accessed, no official research question was replaced, and no submission was made. The next decision should use this qualified contextual baseline and the bounded learning/target-coverage limitations when proposing a scientific claim and any independently frozen confirmation experiment.
