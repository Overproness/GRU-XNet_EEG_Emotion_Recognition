# FACED target, context and EEG-pairing findings

12 October 2026. **Both separately frozen development stages completed and passed independent public numerical audits.** The contextual comparator is now qualified for the known-category information contract. The tested EEG predictors do not demonstrate a consistent incremental advantage. Current manuscripts, the original GRU-XNet research question and protected confirmation remain unchanged.

## What completed

The first stage used 612 Ridge fits, 63 source-prior constructions, 32 donor seeds, 53,760 donor assignments and 107,520 swapped predictions. Independent source-window replay reproduced six EEGNet checkpoints in 24 fixture/seed forwards and 48 offset-convention checks, maximum absolute discrepancy 4.44e-16 against the fixed 1e-5 tolerance. Original source interval/window/QC hashes matched. Validation neural pairing used authenticated cached raw corrections; no independent validation forward replay or whole-recording coverage was performed.

The follow-up used 156 context Ridge fits and 18 prior constructions. It accessed metadata and cached original-scale targets only, with no raw recording, original rating file, private EEG array or neural fitting. Both stages retained every declared validation outcome, including all 15 unavailable EEG trials with their original fallback predictions.

The first stage's 21 guard/replay tests and follow-up's eight tests passed. The second integrity receipt preserved all 20 preceding packet files, existing paper-package bindings and the historical report. Both independent numerical audits use public inputs only and disclose their limits; they do not independently refit Ridge or reproduce switched spectral inputs from private arrays.

## Target variation

Complete role-separated rectangles contain 70 source people and 20 validation people, each with 14 development title families. The exact additive squared-deviation decomposition is:

| Role | Participant component | Title component | Additive residual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Source | 9.95% | 43.29% | 46.76% |
| Validation | 12.83% | 42.31% | 44.86% |

The large title component makes stimulus-aware contextual controls consequential. These are descriptive components, not identified reliability, measurement-noise or response-style estimates. The residual includes person-title interaction and unexplained variation. A single retrospective rating per cell cannot separate those sources or establish a prediction floor.

## A selection limitation and its bounded qualification

Each rotation contains eight source titles, exactly one per assigned category. The first crossed source-person/title folds remove two titles and therefore also remove their categories. Category priors fall back to a global median in every one of the 60 held cells. A category-indicator coefficient cannot be learned for an absent training category. Thus the selected coarse-valence comparator is not a qualified strongest category-informed baseline for the actual held-film setting, where category metadata is known and supported by other source films.

The original results are preserved. A separate activation then qualified contexts using the five fixed source-person folds, each training on 56 people and all eight source titles/categories and assessing 14 held people on those same source titles. This retains category support but estimates held-person performance on familiar source films, **not unseen-title generalization**. It is an adaptive development qualification.

Category medians won source-only selection in every rotation. Both category-only and full-context Ridge selected alpha 0.1 in every rotation. Category, title and nominal duration remain confounded within these one-title/category sources; the ablation cannot identify a causal duration effect or prove semantic category learning.

The original selected spectral-joint and two-seed EEGNet residual anchors were copied unchanged, including context branches and fallbacks. Positive gain means lower MAE than the source-selected category median. Ratings range from 0 to 7.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rotation 0 | 1.2220 | 1.2588 | -0.0368 | 1.1907 | +0.0314 |
| Rotation 1 | 1.2316 | 1.1767 | +0.0549 | 1.3110 | -0.0794 |
| Rotation 2 | 1.3269 | 1.2111 | +0.1158 | 1.2633 | +0.0636 |
| Descriptive mean across rotations | 1.2602 | 1.2156 | +0.0446 | 1.2550 | +0.0052 |

All six unfamiliar-film descriptive crossed gain intervals contain zero. The mean combines dependent sensitivity rotations and is not an independent pooled estimate or confidence interval.

On familiar films, category-median mean MAE is 1.1723, spectral-joint 1.2094 and EEGNet-residual 1.2918. Their mean gains are -0.0372 and -0.1195. The familiar rotation 2 neural gain is -0.1852, with descriptive interval[-0.3498,-0.0143]. This adverse panel is retained; its interval is not a selection-corrected population claim. All other familiar comparison intervals contain zero.

Source-only contextual selection does not undo the EEG anchors' earlier validation selection or reuse of these participants/materials. The earlier 0.15 engineering screen remains unmet for unfamiliar-film gains; it is not a validated perceptual or equivalence margin.

## Matching EEG to the recipient

The first stage held recipient target and context fixed, deranged only available same-arm EEG donors and averaged repeat losses before scoring. Same-film swaps retain acquisition group; other-film swaps retain participant and coarse valence. All 15 unavailable recipients remain fixed fallbacks; singleton strata remain unperturbed and are reported.

| Unfamiliar-film swap loss increase | Rotation 0 | Rotation 1 | Rotation 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Spectral: same film/acquisition, other person | +0.0487 | +0.1017 | +0.1309 |
| EEGNet: same film/acquisition, other person | +0.1221 | -0.0665 | +0.0987 |
| Spectral: same person/coarse valence, other film | +0.0209 | -0.0297 | -0.0160 |
| EEGNet: same person/coarse valence, other film | +0.0625 | -0.0168 | -0.0046 |

Spectral person matching has a consistent direction on unfamiliar titles, while film matching and neural matching are mixed. **All 24 pairing intervals, including familiar panels, contain zero.** This neither proves emotion-specific decoding nor establishes an absence of useful EEG information. Within-person film swaps can retain participant physiology, acquisition characteristics and broad valence. Between-person differences can include artifacts or response tendencies. Those mechanisms are not identified by these controls.

Same-film/person eligibility is 108/107/107 of 120 unfamiliar trials; other-film eligibility 114/113/113. The legacy 1000 validation stratum has one available participant, so its person swaps remain unchanged. Acquisition-stratum summaries are secondary descriptions, with no subgroup significance or complete-crossed subset bootstrap.

## Defensible current claim and remaining concerns

The supported claim is narrow: **in these reused FACED development panels, a source-selected category-median control is competitive with the two tested late-interval EEG predictors; incremental gains and EEG-pairing effects are inconsistent or uncertain.** Category support materially changes the contextual selection comparison. This is an exploratory empirical finding, not a new architecture, a general claim that EEG is uninformative, or a result for all emotion datasets.

The remaining scientific concerns are:

1. **Independent confirmation and precision.** No protected outcome has been opened. Only six unfamiliar title families occur in a development rotation; bootstrap intervals remain wide, and the three rotations are dependent. A null superiority interval cannot establish equivalence.
2. **Stimulus/category confounding.** Category, source title and duration cannot be separately identified within a rotation. Title-family separation is catalogue-derived; independence of the original footage has not been authenticated. More verified independent materials would improve the research design more directly than additional random initializations on the same films.
3. **Target and signal coverage.** Item 10 is retrospective valence; the predictors cover a late 32-second interval. They do not establish momentary affect, full-video utility, rating reliability or richer affect targets. Broader temporal coverage needs a separate bounded access/preprocessing declaration.
4. **Baseline completeness.** Context selection is qualified and source model replay passed. Earlier EEGNet learning curves still left convergence questions. No longer neural schedule, foundation encoder or whole-trial representation was trained in this task. Any paper must bound its conclusions to the evaluated recipes unless separately justified controls are completed.
5. **External validity and novelty.** A single small-stimulus dataset and established predictors do not yet support a strong general methodological contribution. DENS execution/timing joins remain dependent on the authors' codebook; no additional inquiry or dataset fitting occurred here. Historical DEAP authentication remains separate from this FACED study.

The next scientific decision is whether to pursue a narrowly framed evaluation paper with independent confirmation, or keep the current paper explicitly exploratory while qualifying a better-supported public dataset. The accompanying proposal is for author review; it does not change the manuscript or authorize protected access. Further model-grid expansion has no qualifying lead from these results.
