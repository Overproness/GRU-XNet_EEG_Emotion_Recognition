# Transformer and evaluation prior-work update

Date: 6 October 2026. This supplement narrows possible contribution claims while the [fixed-duration transformer/native-label controls](Temporal_Native_Control_Protocol_2026-10-06.md) run. No changed research question is adopted. These are targeted additional sources, not a claim that the literature review is exhaustive.

| Primary work | Verified relationship and implication |
| --- | --- |
| Song et al., **EEG Conformer**, IEEE TNSRE 2023 | Convolution plus transformer attention already has an established EEG classification precedent, including SEED. The author repository asks for consistent training/validation/test splits. Our small bandpower sequence transformer is an independently written control; it is not a reproduction or a new architecture claim. [Author repository and citation](https://github.com/eeyhsong/EEG-Conformer). |
| Li, Li and Zhang, **One Model for All**, arXiv v1, 11 November 2025 | Contrastive univariate pretraining across heterogeneous EEG, a union of channel identities, adaptive transformer tokenization and graph-based multivariate fine-tuning overlap the broad motivation of a universal multi-dataset model. The inspected version's main evaluation is personalized within-subject fine-tuning; its DREAMER transfer table is also explicitly within-subject. It does not provide a directly comparable unseen-participant, target-label-free score. This version describes itself as submitted to IEEE; acceptance was not verified. [Full primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08444v1). |
| **PESD**, Expert Systems with Applications 2025 | Pretrained Vision Transformer transfer is already explored on SEED, SEED-IV, DEAP and FACED. The author repository provides pretrained models and says each is trained on a participant selected for emotional sensitivity. Exact participant selection and target access must be audited before reproduction or numerical comparison. We have inspected the primary publisher text and repository README, but have not reproduced the method. [Publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417425009704), [author repository](https://github.com/fangwangeeg/PESD). |
| Hu et al., **A Novel Experiment Setting for Cross-subject Emotion Recognition**, EMBC 2021 | The authors explicitly study shared emotion-induction videos and evaluate both unseen people and different stimulus materials. For SEED-IV, different sessions provide different clip sets; their off-diagonal evaluation couples new sessions/materials. Subject-disjoint evaluation alone does not establish new-stimulus generalization. Their KPCA control uses target covariates, so its numerical results also require target-access qualification. This issue predates our exploration. [Primary conference proceedings paper](https://www.paperhost.org/proceedings/embs/EMBC21/files/1565.pdf). |
| Suo and Li, **Evaluation Protocols and Cross-Subject Generalization in EEG Emotion Recognition**, arXiv v1, 30 July 2026 | This preprint directly examines checkpoint selection, participant-disjoint performance, statistical units and separate final evaluation on SEED/SEED-IV. Its SEED-IV reproduction compatibility discrepancy remains unresolved and is acknowledged by the authors. A generic claim that strict evaluation lowers accuracy or that held-out participants are harder is therefore insufficient novelty. [Full primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27655v1). |

These sources strengthen two practical decisions. First, adding a transformer should be a matched baseline experiment before treating it as a contribution. Second, if a promising representation or label objective emerges, evaluate it on both held-out participants and held-out stimulus material before interpreting it as robust emotion recognition. A SEED-IV session-separated test changes recording conditions and videos together; it cannot by itself isolate the causal influence of videos.

The current local observation is that SEED-IV trial duration differs by the original emotion labels. The predeclared sequence experiment takes the first 40 seconds of every original trial and adds a duration-only logistic diagnostic. This removes explicit input-length variation and quantifies one shortcut association. It does not prove the old model exploited duration, establish a newly discovered field-wide issue, or separate stimulus-evoked sensory responses from emotional responses. Both the fixed-duration architecture comparison and any future session/material comparison remain development experiments.

The earlier [mdJPT/REVE research assessment](GRU-XNet_Neural_Transfer_Investigation_2026-10-05.md) remains relevant. Our current control trains a small transformer from scratch on bandpower windows; it does not evaluate a pretrained EEG foundation model. We still need to audit checkpoint pretraining overlap, target labels/covariates, electrode mapping, sampling-rate requirements and actual 6 GB GPU feasibility before reporting a foundation-model result.

No manuscript source changes follow from this review. A strong-conference proposal still needs a specific gap beyond these precedents, controlled evidence across datasets and protocols, and a fresh confirmatory evaluation or an explicitly exploratory framing. There is no acceptance guarantee from choosing a newer architecture.
