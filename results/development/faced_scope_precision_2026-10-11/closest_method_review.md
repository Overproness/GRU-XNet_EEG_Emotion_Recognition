## Stimulus and timing claim boundary

The already-public successful numeric projection verifies all 3,444 original marker spans and the fixed clip joins. Among 2,829 included technical spans, the shortest is 34.106 seconds, leaving nominal marker-relative margins of 2.106 seconds before the 32-second input and 4.106 seconds before the analyzed core. Native integer endpoint flooring alone can reduce those margins by less than four milliseconds, leaving conservative lower bounds of 2.102/4.102 seconds. The core ends two marker-seconds before the end marker. Included marker duration minus nominal catalogue duration spans −0.479 to +0.802 seconds. These differences are **not measured playback latency**.

Clips 7/8 share *The Shining* and remain one protected family; neutral clips share *Blue*; clip 22 has two marker-duration versions and stays excluded globally. Other titles are distinct under the frozen catalogue normalization. The catalogue sources include FilmStim, PED, DCEF and THU-EP, so reuse/pretraining exposure is plausible and unauthenticated. Exact-title grouping does not exclude thematically related content or duplicate footage in different catalogues. Workspace inventories found metadata and preprocessing/validation code, but did not authenticate the original stimulus movies, playback execution log or physical synchronization measurements. No family or person was reassigned to improve observed results.

The resolved scope is **offline marker-relative late EEG → retrospective individual valence, conditional on supplied study metadata, transferred between catalogue source titles**. Claims about physical footage independence, exact content alignment, frame-specific emotion or arbitrary novel media remain outside scope. Narrowing those claims is feasible; authenticating stronger claims would require original media/execution records. This is distinct from the unresolved EmoEEG-MC event/rating codebook dependency.

## Precision source boundary

[Westfall, Kenny and Judd (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580/) establishes why participant/stimulus variation must both enter study design and why a finite stimulus sample can limit power as participant count increases. The [Owen/Eckles multiway bootstrap paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2125) gives a primary theoretical basis for crossed resampling under its assumptions; it does not guarantee our eight-family coverage. The present ANOVA projections are an explicitly assumed additive working model, not a reproduced power procedure or validated small-cluster inferential method. The proposed primary interval is conditional on the fixed material roster, with broader material resampling labeled descriptive.
